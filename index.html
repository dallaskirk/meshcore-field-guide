THE 2-MINUTE EXPLAINER

Okay, but what is mesh?

Imagine a group text where the messages travel through tiny independent radios instead of cell towers.

One message, several possible paths

👤You + phoneType a message
→
📻Your companionSends LoRa packet
→
🏔️RepeaterForwards it
→
💬RecipientReads the text

MeshCore selects or discovers paths using compatible nodes and repeaters. Messages only get through when usable radio links and routing exist; a nationwide chain is a possibility to build toward, not guaranteed coverage.

📶

LoRa = long-range radio

LoRa sends small amounts of data over radio frequencies. Its strength is low-power, long-distance communication under favorable conditions—not fast data.

🔁

Mesh = connected helpers

Repeaters listen and relay messages to extend range. They don't create magic coverage through mountains and buildings; placement matters.

🔐

Encryption ≠ invisibility

Direct messages can be end-to-end encrypted, and private channels use shared secrets. Public / hashtag channels are not private. Radio activity and metadata may still be observable.

🌐

MeshCore ≠ Meshtastic

Two different radio protocols. Some hardware can run either one with appropriate firmware, but they do not directly exchange messages. Choose MeshCore for MountainWest's MeshCore network.

THREE JOBS, THREE DEVICE ROLES

Know which node you're buying.

📱CompanionCarry it. Pair to phone. Message.
🔁RepeaterLeave it placed high. Relay.
🏠Room serverHost a chat space.

Different firmware roles. Your first purchase should almost certainly be a companion radio, not a rooftop repeater.

DIY note: The microcontroller board (like Heltec or RAK) does the actual MeshCore radio work. Raspberry Pi is an optional Linux computer for custom software or management. You do not need a Pi, Wi-Fi, or cellular service to send basic MeshCore radio messages.
Do I need the internet at any point? To buy the device and initially download apps/firmware, yes. Once installed and configured, radio-to-radio messaging itself does not need cell service or Wi-Fi. Online maps and some third-party app features may still need internet.

YOUR WORKBENCH GUIDE

From components to first message.

01

Unbox & inspect

Check the exact 915 MHz version, revision, board, antenna, USB cable and enclosure fit. Keep the battery disconnected during initial inspection.

02

Attach antenna, bench-power by USB

Use the included antenna connector carefully—tiny U.FL/IPEX plugs are fragile. Plug in a USB data cable to the computer. Do not transmit without the antenna.

03

Flash the correct firmware

Open the MeshCore web flasher ↗ in Chrome/Edge; select Heltec V3 and BLE Companion Radio (not Repeater). Exact menu labels may vary.

04

Pair & apply the regional profile

Install the phone app, pair over Bluetooth, and apply MountainWest settings ↗. Check the current 2-byte path hash standard.

05

Add your battery safely

Only after verifying battery voltage and connector polarity for your revision, disconnect USB and install the battery. Never force the connector, short the terminals, or leave a suspect cell charging.

06

Finish the printed case & test

Keep the antenna routed safely, avoid pinched cables, close the case, and repeat with the second radio. Turn cellular/Wi-Fi off on the phones and send a test message.

Battery safety: Connector shape alone does not guarantee polarity. LiPo cells can be damaged or ignite if miswired, crushed, overcharged, or shorted. If you’re uncertain, skip the battery and do your first session powered by USB until the board/vendor wiring is verified.

THE RASPBERRY PI QUESTION

Where does a Raspberry Pi fit?

🥧

The Pi is the optional brain

Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, Pi 4, and Pi 5 are tiny Linux computers. They have Wi-Fi/Bluetooth but no 915 MHz MeshCore LoRa transceiver built in. They do not talk to MountainWest Mesh by radio on their own.

Official MeshCore docs describe flashing a supported radio and managing a repeater over USB serial from a Pi. That’s useful for diagnostics, automation and custom code—not necessary for your first pair of Bluetooth handhelds.

Read MeshCore Pi instructions ↗

A practical Pi-powered project

1
Raspberry Pi + SD card + power

A Pi Zero 2 W can be a compact Linux controller; USB OTG adapters may be needed to connect a radio.

2
Separate compatible 915 MHz LoRa board

Use a Heltec V3 or RAK4631 running firmware appropriate for the radio’s job, connected over USB.

3
Write the software layer

Explore command-line management, logging, integrations or a custom map. Capabilities depend on the device firmware and software you choose.

Suggested build order: Don’t buy a Raspberry Pi yet unless the fun part is writing software. Build two working Heltecs first, then decide whether the next experiment is a GPS-equipped handheld, a custom friends map, or a home repeater. The Pi becomes a meaningful upgrade only for some of those projects.

RAK4631 WisBlock for tinkerers.

Typical minimum hardware

1

2
Compatible WisBlock base board

RAK19007 is one example; supplies USB/charging connections and module slots.

3
LoRa antenna + BLE antenna

Verify both are included or order matching parts, plus safe power/battery and a case.

4
Optional GNSS module

Not part of the bare RAK4631/core + base. GPS capabilities and software compatibility need separate verification.

Why consider it?

A modular base makes sense if you want to attach additional hardware or optimize power consumption. You will still flash MeshCore Companion firmware and use the same MountainWest radio settings.

RAK’s listed base board price is around $9.99 (November 18, 2026); the core, antennas, battery, enclosure, and any GNSS hardware are additional. Shop the right 915 MHz version.

THE FESTIVAL REALITY CHECK

Location sharing isn't automatic

A GPS chip tells your device where it is. Another person only sees that position if your chosen app/software actually sends it over the mesh and they successfully receive it.

Privacy first: Avoid public location advertisements if you're trying to keep your whereabouts within a trusted group. Use explicitly consent-based private location-sharing tools, and understand their behavior before an event.
Explore MeshCore TEAM ↗

MeshCore TEAM is a third-party team-tracking app; not required for basic MeshCore messages. Verify current compatibility with your phones and radio firmware.

Don't use this as your only safety lifeline. Festival crowd density, physical obstacles, batteries, and regional settings can interrupt delivery. Keep agreed meeting spots and conventional emergency plans too.

HELP / NOTES / LINKS

Good questions, good answers.

The stuff everyone wonders before committing to their first tiny radio.

Frequently asked questions

Will it work from Salt Lake City to Idaho or St. George?

Possibly, if there is a working path through compatible repeaters and the regional routing settings allow that traffic. A map shows known infrastructure—not a promise that messages will get through between any two locations.

Can I build the whole thing using just a Raspberry Pi?

Not with a normal Raspberry Pi alone. It has Wi‑Fi/Bluetooth but not the required long-range LoRa transceiver. Start with a MeshCore-supported LoRa board (Heltec V3 or RAK4631, for example). A Raspberry Pi can optionally flash/manage that radio over USB or act as a controller for your own software. The simplest phone companion setup uses no Pi at all. See official MeshCore FAQ ↗

Do I need one radio per person?

Yes, if each person wants their own independent off-grid connection. The smartphone app alone does not turn a phone into a LoRa transmitter.

What if I buy a Meshtastic radio?

Some compatible radio hardware can be reflashed with MeshCore. But the protocols are separate. For the simplest experience, buy the preflashed MeshCore edition and verify it supports the US band.

Do I have to buy a rooftop repeater?

No. Start with a portable companion. A repeater helps when there's a useful radio path it can extend. It isn't a requirement to join, and one in a poor location may add little benefit.

Are private messages really private?

MeshCore direct messages are designed for end-to-end encryption. A private channel uses a shared secret key, so protect that key. Hashtag and public channels use predictable or broadly shared keys; don't put sensitive information on them. Other radio traffic metadata may still be observable.

Will it show exactly where my friends are?

Not by default. Accurate location sharing needs GPS or phone location, a compatible app with tracking features, opt-in permissions, and message delivery. Tracking frequency, map availability, and background behavior vary by software and device.

Why don't I see any nodes?

Double-check 910.525 MHz / 62.5 kHz / SF7 / CR5 and the current 2-byte path setting. Go near a window or outside, check the antenna, let the device discover advertisements, inspect the live/coverage maps and ask local community members for a test contact.

Does this require an amateur radio license?

MountainWest describes its US network as unlicensed-band LoRa use. That isn't a blanket exemption from regulation: use appropriate US-compliant equipment and follow current FCC requirements and local mounting rules, especially for boosted power or outdoor antenna systems.

How current are the prices and settings?

We checked the cited community pages and Seeed listing on November 18, 2026. Prices and firmware/settings can change. Always confirm the official device listing and MountainWest settings page before purchasing or flashing.

Microcontrollers that run MeshCore firmware on supported boards.

A LoRa radio chip found in many supported boards.

A radio that connects to your phone to send and receive texts.

Flash

Technology for knowing a device's geographic position.
Path hash
Data MeshCore uses when describing message paths through repeaters.
